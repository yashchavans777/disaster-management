

## Official Location of Aizawl (With Coordinates)

**City:** Aizawl  
**State:** Mizoram  
**Country:** India  
**Coordinates:** 23°43′59″N (23.733°N), 92°43′05″E (92.718°E)[1][2][3][4][5]
**District:** Aizawl District[6][7][8]
**PIN Codes:** 796001 (Aizawl), 796012 (Ramhlun), 796014 (Bawngkawn), 796025 (Durtlang), 796009 (Vaivakawn), 796471 (Zawlnuam), 796005 (Kulikawn), 796036 (Sihphir), 796230 (Sialsuk), 796017 (Zemabawk), 796190 (Aibawk), 796007 (Chandmary), 796161 (Thingsulthliah), 796111 (Darlawn), 796015 (Tlangnuam)[6]
**Elevation:** 1,132 meters (3,714 feet) above sea level, situated on a ridge with Tlawng river valley to its west and Tuirial river valley to its east[1][4][7]
**Seismic Zone:** Zone V (highest seismic hazard)[6]
**Landslide Susceptibility:** High (entire district classified as highly vulnerable to landslides)[6][9]
**Flood Hazard Zone:** Moderate (due to hilly terrain and river valleys)[6]

**District Profile:**
- **Area:** 3,576 square kilometers[6]
- **Population (2011 Census):** 400,309 (Aizawl District); 293,416 (Aizawl City)[1][2][3][6]
- **Literacy Rate:** 98.21% (District); 98.36% (City)[6]
- **Climate:** Tropical Monsoon Type; average annual rainfall: 2,786.6 mm[6]
- **Temperature Range:** Maximum 36.7°C (April); Minimum 5.4°C (December)[6]

## **2021**

**Source:** Aizawl District Disaster Management Plan 2022; Mizoram SDMA reports; IMD DWE 2021[6][11][13][18]

### **2021 (Specific Dates Not Fully Documented in IMD Summary)**

**Landslides & Heavy Rainfall:**
- **Villages Affected (Aizawl District, 2021-2022):** 112 villages affected by landslides during the 2021-2022 period.[13]
- **Human Casualties (Aizawl District, 2021-2022):** 6 deaths, 28 injuries reported due to landslides.[13]
- **Houses Damaged (Aizawl District, 2021-2022):**
  - Fully damaged: 7 houses
  - Severely damaged: 19 houses
  - Partially damaged: Not specified[13]

**People Affected:**

**Roads Affected:**
- Multiple district roads and inter-village roads blocked due to landslides during monsoon 2021.[6][11]
- National Highway 54 and NH-150 experienced intermittent closures due to landslides.[6][11]

**Area Affected:**
- 112 villages affected across Aizawl District during 2021-2022.[13]
- Multiple localities in Aizawl city reported landslides damaging houses and infrastructure.[6][11]

**Financial Assistance Distributed (2021-2022):**
- Rs 62,01,264/- distributed as financial assistance for disaster relief in Aizawl District.[6]

**Reasons for Disasters:**
- Heavy monsoon rainfall triggered landslides in the fragile Himalayan terrain.[6][9][11]
- Unplanned construction and slope modification in landslide-prone areas increased vulnerability.[6][9]
- Deforestation and land-use changes reducing the land's capacity to absorb rainfall.[6][9]

## **2024**

**Source:** IMD Disastrous Weather Events 2024; Mizoram Government Press Releases; Local News Reports; Academic Studies[1][9][10][14][15][16][19]

### **24–28 May 2024: Cyclone Remal**

**Cyclone Remal Overview:**
- **Formation:** 24 May 2024 over the north and adjoining east-central Bay of Bengal[10]
- **Intensification:** Intensified into a Severe Cyclonic Storm on 26 May 2024[10]
- **Landfall:** Crossed Bangladesh and adjoining West Bengal coasts between 26-27 May 2024[10]
- **Impact on Mizoram:** Brought intense rainfall to Mizoram, triggering catastrophic landslides in Aizawl and other districts.[1][9][10][14][15][16][19]

### **28 May 2024 (Tuesday) – Catastrophic Landslides in Aizawl**

**Location:** Aizawl City (multiple localities)[1][9][14][15][16][19]

**Event Type:** Cyclone Remal-induced landslides and floods[1][9][10][14][15][16][19]

**Time:** Intense rainfall throughout the day; landslides occurred during daytime and evening[1][9]

**Casualties (Aizawl District):**
- **Total Deaths:** 34 deaths reported in Mizoram due to Cyclone Remal, with 27-30 deaths in Aizawl district alone.[1][9][10][14][15][16][19]
- **Breakdown by Locality (as of 7 PM, 28 May 2024):**[1]
  - Melthum: 14 deceased
  - Hlimen: 4 deceased
  - Salem: 3 deceased
  - Falkawn: 2 deceased
  - Lungsei: 1 deceased
  - Kelsih: 1 deceased
  - Aibawk: 2 deceased
- **Injured:** 25 others injured (from 6 May 2023 landslide; 2024 injury count not separately specified)[9][12]
- **Missing:** 11 missing (from 6 May 2023 landslide; 2024 missing count not separately specified)[12]

**People Affected:**
- **Families Affected:** 72 families affected by landslides in Aizawl.[9]
- **Houses Evacuated:** 54 houses evacuated within Aizawl.[9][15]
- **Houses Damaged (State-wide, March-May 2024):** 5,938 houses and buildings destroyed due to disasters in Mizoram.[16]
- **Cemeteries Affected:** 4 localities' cemeteries affected; more than 300 graves displaced.[9]

**Areas Affected (Aizawl City):**
- Melthum[1][9][14]
- Hlimen[9][14][1]
- Falkawn[1][14]
- Salem Veng[1][14]
- Lungsei[1]
- Kelsih[1]
- Aibawk[1]
- Laipuitland locality (from 6 May 2023 event)[12]

**Roads Affected:**
- Multiple district roads and inter-village roads blocked due to landslides.[6][11]
- National Highway 54 and NH-150 experienced closures due to landslides.[6][11]
- Specific road damage data for 28 May 2024 event not separately published in IMD 2024 report.[10]

**Infrastructure Damage:**
- **Power Supply:** Power and Electricity (P&E) Department issued notices stating that electricity supply would be stifled until further notice as electric lines were damaged by landslides and floods.[1]
- **Water Supply:** Public Health Engineering (PHE) Department issued notices stating that water supply would be stifled until further notice as water pumps were damaged by landslides and floods.[1]
- **Houses Damaged (Melthum and Hlimen areas):** 10 houses damaged.[9]
- **Vehicles Damaged (Melthum and Hlimen areas):** 22 vehicles damaged.[9]
- **Total Property Loss (Melthum and Hlimen areas):** Estimated at Rs 3.4 crores.[9]
- **Cemeteries:** More than 300 graves displaced.[9]

**Relief Efforts:**
- **State Disaster Relief Fund (SDRF):** Chief Minister Lalduhoma announced a State Disaster Relief Fund of ₹15 crore.[1]
- **Ex-Gratia:** ₹4 lakh as ex-gratia for the deceased (₹2 lakhs to be given immediately, remaining half later as per SDRF norms).[1]
- **Search and Rescue:** State Disaster Response Force (SDRF) personnel teamed up with Melthum Local Council and YMA (Young Mizo Association) for search and rescue operations.[1]
- **On-Site Officials:** Chief Minister Lalduhoma and Minister in charge of Disaster Management and Rehabilitation (DM&R) K. Sapdanga were on-site at Melthum with other government officials.[1]

### **State-Wide Impact (March-May 2024)**
- **Total Deaths (Mizoram, March-May 2024):** 42 deaths due to calamities.[16]
- **Houses Destroyed (State-wide, March-May 2024):** 5,938 houses and buildings destroyed.[16]
- **Central Assistance Sought:** State government sought Rs 237 crore from the Centre for rehabilitation and infrastructure repair.[16]

### **IMD DWE 2024 Data (Mizoram State-wide)**
- **Total Deaths (Mizoram, 2024):** 34 deaths[10]
  - Lightning/Thunderstorm: 1 death
  - Floods/Heavy Rains: 3 deaths
  - Cyclonic Storm (Remal): 30 deaths[10]
- **Total Deaths (Mizoram, 2024, Monsoon Season):** 34 deaths (all attributed to Cyclone Remal and associated landslides/floods)[10]

**Reasons for Disasters:**
- Intense rainfall from Cyclone Remal triggered landslides in the fragile Himalayan terrain.[1][9][10][14][15][16][19]
- Lack of expert consultation on slope modifications, weak rock mass characteristics, steep slopes, inadequate infrastructure planning, and fragile geological conditions increased the likelihood of slope failure.[9]
- Unplanned construction and slope modification in landslide-prone areas.[6][9]
- Climate change-induced intensification of extreme rainfall events.[9]

## Additional Context from Aizawl District Disaster Management Plan 2022

**Hazard, Vulnerability, Capacity and Risk Assessment (HVCRA):**
- **Landslide Hazard:** Aizawl District is classified as highly vulnerable to landslides due to steep slopes, fragile geology, and heavy monsoon rainfall.[6][9]
- **Flood Hazard:** Moderate flood hazard due to hilly terrain and river valleys (Tlawng and Tuirial).[6]
- **Earthquake Hazard:** Seismic Zone V (highest seismic hazard).[6]
- **Cyclone Hazard:** Moderate cyclone hazard; cyclones from the Bay of Bengal can bring intense rainfall triggering landslides.[6][9][10]

**Historical Disaster Matrix (Aizawl District):**[6]

| Type of Disaster | Year | No. of Villages Affected | Houses Damaged | Animals Perished | Human Casualty (Lives Lost) | Human Casualty (Injury) | Financial Assistance |
|-----------------|------|-------------------------|----------------|-----------------|----------------------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| Landslide | 2020-2021 | Not specified | Not specified | Nil | 1 | Not specified | Rs 49,76,255/- |
| Landslide | 2021-2022 | 28 | 14 | Nil | 6 | Nil | Rs 62,01,264/- |
| Fire | 2021-2022 | 12 | 23 | Nil | 1 | Nil | Rs 20,83,060/- |
| Cyclone | 2021-2022 | 25 | 33 | Nil | Nil | Nil | Rs 25,08,375/- |

**Institutional Arrangements:**
- **Aizawl District Disaster Management Authority (ADDMA):** Chaired by Deputy Commissioner, Aizawl District.[6]
- **District Emergency Operations Centre (DEOC):** Operational for 24x7 coordination during disasters.[6]
- **Emergency Support Functions (ESFs):** 13 ESFs organized for disaster response (including search and rescue, medical relief, infrastructure restoration, etc.).[6]

**Relief and Rehabilitation:**
- **Shelter Sites:** Multiple shelter sites identified across Aizawl District for disaster relief.[6]
- **Medical Facilities:** Civil Hospital, Aizawl and multiple primary health centers available for disaster response.[6]
- **Resource Inventory:** Stockpiles of relief materials, rescue equipment, and emergency supplies maintained.[6]
