*2024 flood reason in silchar city*-

The primary reason for the 2024 flood in Silchar city was an extremely concentrated, high-intensity rainfall pattern driven by severe meteorological events, amplified by the geographic layout of the Barak Valley.
1. Pre-Monsoon Extreme Spikes (Late April 2024)
 * Pre-Monsoon Thunderstorm Squalls: In late April, Silchar experienced high-intensity, short-duration downpours accompanied by squally winds.
 * Pre-Saturation of Soil: This initial surge saturated the ground soil early, reducing the land's natural water-absorption capacity before the actual monsoon arrived.
2. Tropical Cyclone Remal & Concentrated Downpours (May 28–31, 2024)
 * Short-Window Hyper-Rainfall: Following the landfall of Cyclone Remal, Silchar and its surrounding catchments received over 400 mm of rainfall in a 48-hour window.
 * Single-Day Peak Spike: On May 31, 2024, Cachar district recorded an unprecedented 24-hour rainfall spike of 415.8 mm, far exceeding standard daily seasonal averages.
3. Upstream Orographic Catchment Rainfall (Dima Hasao & Meghalaya Hills)
 * Catchment Runoff Effect: The rainfall pattern was not restricted to Silchar town itself. Extreme rainfall hit the upstream hilly catchments of Dima Hasao and the Meghalaya Hills.
 * Rapid Downslope Discharge: Because of steep terrain, this massive runoff rushed down simultaneously into the Barak River and its tributaries (Madhura, Jatinga, and Sonai), overwhelming the river systems in hours.
4. Swelling of the Barak River Above Danger Level
 * Rapid Gauge Rise: The intense rainfall pattern caused the Barak River at Annapurna Ghat (Silchar) to surge above its critical danger mark (19.83 meters), reaching peak flood stages within hours.
 * Backwater Effect: Because the primary river channels were flowing above capacity, municipal drains and local channels could not discharge urban runoff into the Barak River, causing water to back up into Silchar city.

9) Reasons of Floods in 2021 – Silchar and Cachar
Heavy monsoon rainfall (May–August 2021; multiple Yellow/Orange alerts).

Barak River water level rise above Danger Level (Annapurnaghat gauge).

Embankment weakness and inundation (Berenga–Betukandi zone; seepage and over‑topping).

Blocked cross‑drainage structures (NH‑6 corridor, internal drains, nullahs).

Riverbed siltation and reduced river capacity (Barak River and tributaries).

Upstream water release and runoff from hills (Manipur, Mizoram, Meghalaya; tributaries Jiri, Chiri, Madhura, Jatinga, Sonai, Katakhal).

Urban planning issues and encroachments (unplanned construction, drain encroachments, weak embankments).

Terrain and landscape factors (alluvial flat plain, swamps, streams, small hills “Tilla”).

Seismic Zone V vulnerability (flood‑damaged structures more vulnerable to earthquakes)
