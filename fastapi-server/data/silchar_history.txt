Historical Disaster Data for Silchar and NER:
Location: Silchar is located in the southernmost part of Assam.
Longitude: 92°24′ E to 93°15′ E.
Latitude: 24°22′ N to 25°08′ N.
Elevation: Approximately 35 metres above mean sea level.
Terrain: Silchar lies on an alluvial flat plain.
Landscape: The area contains:
Swamps
Streams
Small isolated hills locally called “Tilla”
Major Rivers:
Barak River
Ghagra River
Seismic Zone: Silchar falls under Seismic Zone V, indicating a very high earthquake-risk area.
Historical Earthquake Risk: Silchar has experienced several major earthquakes.
Major Earthquakes:
1869: Magnitude 7.5 – caused heavy damage.
1947: Magnitude 7.7.
1957: Magnitude 7.0.
1984: Magnitude 6.0.
Disaster Management Importance: Due to its Zone V seismic classification, low-lying alluvial terrain, swamps, streams, and river systems, Silchar requires strong earthquake preparedness, flood management, early-warning systems, evacuation planning, and emergency response infrastructure.
*2022 flood report-
 1) Basic profile – Silchar city and Barak River
City: Silchar (headquarters of Cachar district, Assam, Barak Valley).

Approximate coordinates: 24.82° N, 92.80° E; elevation ~22 m above MSL.

Setting: Located on the Barak River floodplain; main town lies about 1 km from the Barak River embankment that failed in 2022.

Administrative units: Cachar district has 5 revenue circles – Silchar Sadar, Lakhipur, Sonai, Katigorah, Udharbond; 198 of 867 villages are classified as flood‑prone.

2) Barak River – storage / discharge context (official basin data)
Basin: Barak and Others Basin (covers parts of Assam, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, Nagaland).

Average annual water potential: 48.36 BCM (billion cubic metres).

Mean annual rainfall (basin): ~2,083.5 mm; some north‑western and south‑western parts receive 5,000–6,000 mm annually.

Maximum recorded discharge (Barak stretch): about 7,786 m³/s (used in planning documents).

Key gauge near Silchar: Annapurnaghat (Silchar) on Barak River; Highest Flood Level (HFL) recorded 21.84 m in 1989.

Storage note: There is no major upstream reservoir directly regulating Barak flows at Silchar; flood peaks are mainly due to monsoon runoff from hills and tributaries (Jiri, Chiri, Madhura, Jatinga, Sonai, Katakhal, etc.).

3) 2022 Silchar flood – timeline with affected areas (date & time wise)
First flood wave (May 2022)
14 May 2022: Barak River crosses danger level (DL) for the first time in the season.

19 May 2022: Embankment breach at Berenga (Berenga point) upstream of Silchar; floodwater enters Mahisha Beel / Maheesha Beel and low‑lying peripheries.

23 May 2022: River level falls below DL, ending the first wave, but the breach leaves the system vulnerable.

Affected areas (first wave):

Villages across Silchar Sadar, Lakhipur, Sonai, Katigorah, Udharbond revenue circles reported affected.

Berenga–Mahisha Beel corridor identified as a key entry point for floodwater into Silchar’s low‑lying zones.

Second flood wave – main urban flood (June 2022)
18 June 2022: Barak River again crosses danger level (from ~19.62 m to 20.98 m in 24 hours).

19 June 2022 (~midnight): Major dyke/embankment breach at Bethukandi (Bethkundi) on Barak River, ~1 km from Silchar town; town begins to submerge.

19–30 June 2022: Silchar remains under water for ~11 days; water depth up to 12 ft (≈3.7 m) in some localities; roughly 90% of the town reported inundated.

Urban areas affected (June 2022, official list):

26 out of 28 wards of Silchar Town affected.

Specific localities named in ASDMA/DDMA reports as severely impacted on 26 June 2022:

National Highway road (NH corridor through Silchar)

Ambicapatty

Church Road

Chandicharan Road

Bilpar

Public School Road

Subhas Nagar

N.S. Avenue

Kanakpur

These areas fall mainly under Silchar Sadar revenue circle, which recorded 196 affected villages in the 2022 cumulative count.

Extended inundation (July 2022)
By mid‑July 2022: ISRO/NRSC flood maps confirm extensive inundation across Cachar, including Silchar, Lakhipur, Katigorah, Udharbond, Sonai circles.

Cumulative (14 May–30 June 2022):

607 of 868 villages in Cachar affected.

Revenue‑circle breakdown (villages affected):

Silchar Sadar: 196

Lakhipur: 58

Sonai: 161

Katigorah: 152

Udharbond: 40

4) National Highway affected – exact identification
National Highway affected: NH 6 (old numbering; now part of renumbered NH 27 corridor) passing through Silchar.

Affected stretch: The Silchar town section along the National Highway road is explicitly listed among the worst‑hit urban areas in ASDMA’s 26 June 2022 flood report.

Nature of impact:

Urban flooding along the NH 6 corridor due to blocked cross‑drainage, over‑topping/weak embankments, and water entering from breached sections near the city.

Resulted in prolonged waterlogging and traffic disruption on the Silchar–NH 6 stretch during 19–30 June 2022.

5) Human impact summary (for project background)
Population of Cachar district: ~17.36 lakh.

People affected (district‑wide, 2022): ~14.31 lakh (1,431,654).

Urban impact: 26/28 wards of Silchar Town affected.

Evacuations (19–29 June 2022): About 70,014 people shifted to relief camps.

Relief camps: 292 relief centers / camps operated in the district.

14 May 2022: Barak crosses danger level – first flood wave begins.

19 May 2022: Berenga embankment breach → floodwater enters Mahisha Beel and nearby low areas.

23 May 2022: River falls below danger level – first wave ends.

18 June 2022: Barak again crosses danger level – second wave starts.

19 June 2022 (~midnight): Bethukandi dyke breach (~1 km from Silchar) → town starts submerging.
