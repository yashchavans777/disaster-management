*2022 flood report-
 1) Basic profile – Silchar city and Barak River
City: Silchar (headquarters of Cachar district, Assam, Barak Valley).

Approximate coordinates: 24.82° N, 92.80° E; elevation ~22 m above MSL.

Setting: Located on the Barak River floodplain; main town lies about 1 km from the Barak River embankment that failed in 2022.

Administrative units: Cachar district has 5 revenue circles – Silchar Sadar, Lakhipur, Sonai, Katigorah, Udharbond; 198 of 867 villages are classified as flood‑prone.

2) Barak River – storage / discharge context (official basin data)
Basin: Barak and Others Basin (covers parts of Assam, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura, Meghalaya, Nagaland).

Average annual water potential: 48.36 BCM (billion cubic metres).

Mean annual rainfall (basin): ~2,083.5 mm; some north‑western and south‑western parts receive 5,000–6,000 mm annually.

Maximum recorded discharge (Barak stretch): about 7,786 m³/s (used in planning documents).

Key gauge near Silchar: Annapurnaghat (Silchar) on Barak River; Highest Flood Level (HFL) recorded 21.84 m in 1989.

Storage note: There is no major upstream reservoir directly regulating Barak flows at Silchar; flood peaks are mainly due to monsoon runoff from hills and tributaries (Jiri, Chiri, Madhura, Jatinga, Sonai, Katakhal, etc.).

3) 2022 Silchar flood – timeline with affected areas (date & time wise)
First flood wave (May 2022)
14 May 2022: Barak River crosses danger level (DL) for the first time in the season.

19 May 2022: Embankment breach at Berenga (Berenga point) upstream of Silchar; floodwater enters Mahisha Beel / Maheesha Beel and low‑lying peripheries.

23 May 2022: River level falls below DL, ending the first wave, but the breach leaves the system vulnerable.

Affected areas (first wave):

Villages across Silchar Sadar, Lakhipur, Sonai, Katigorah, Udharbond revenue circles reported affected.

Berenga–Mahisha Beel corridor identified as a key entry point for floodwater into Silchar’s low‑lying zones.

Second flood wave – main urban flood (June 2022)
18 June 2022: Barak River again crosses danger level (from ~19.62 m to 20.98 m in 24 hours).

19 June 2022 (~midnight): Major dyke/embankment breach at Bethukandi (Bethkundi) on Barak River, ~1 km from Silchar town; town begins to submerge.

19–30 June 2022: Silchar remains under water for ~11 days; water depth up to 12 ft (≈3.7 m) in some localities; roughly 90% of the town reported inundated.

Urban areas affected (June 2022, official list):

26 out of 28 wards of Silchar Town affected.

*disaster faced by Silchar in 2022 – the pre‑monsoon thunderstorm / hailstorm -
1) Event Overview & Meteorological Parameters

Disaster Classification: Severe Pre-Monsoon Thunderstorm, Hailstorm, and High-Speed Squall Line (Local Term: Kalbaishakhi).

Primary Occurrence Window: April 14 – April 16, 2022 (Pre-monsoon peak convective season).

Peak Wind Speeds: Recorded at 65–85 km/h during localized microbursts and squall lines.

Precipitation Intensity: Rainfall surpassed 120 mm within a 12-hour window, accompanied by severe localized hailstorms across Cachar district.

2) Human, Household & Agricultural Impact Data

Affected District Population: ∼85,000–110,000 individuals across urban and rural belts in Cachar.

Human Casualties: 4 recorded fatalities (caused by wall collapses, uprooted trees, and electrocution from fallen power cables) and over 35 reported injuries.

Residential Infrastructure Damage:

Fully Destroyed Structures: ∼1,200+ kutcha houses and temporary roofs.

Partially Damaged Structures: ∼4,500+ residential units, commercial tin sheds, and signboards.

Agricultural Impact: Over 3,200 hectares of standing pre-kharif crops and horticultural plantations (paddy, vegetables, betel nut orchards) flattened across Cachar.

3) City-Level Affected Localities (Silchar Town)

Tarapur & Railway Station Zone: Massive tree uprooting and roof damage causing temporary blockage of station access roads.

Fatak Bazar (Main Commercial Hub): Heavy structural damage to temporary shop stalls, signboards, and commercial roofing; localized flash waterlogging.

Ambicapatty, Link Road, & Rangirkhari: High-voltage transmission wire snags and downed power poles resulting in a 36-hour urban power blackout.

Srikona & Silchar Bypass Belt: Heavy tree branches and structural debris blocked primary municipal arterial corridors.

4) Affected National Highways & Transport Corridors

Primary Affected National Highway: NH-6 (Connecting Silchar to Meghalaya and Guwahati) and NH-27 (East-West Corridor connecting Silchar to Haflong/Lumding via Dima Hasao).

Exact Impacted Stretches:

NH-6 (Silchar–Badarpur Section): Disrupted traffic movement due to multiple uprooted large banyan trees and downed electricity poles spanning across the road lanes.

NH-27 (Silchar–Udarbond–Kalain Stretch): Traffic blocked by debris from roadside structures and localized soil erosion along shoulder embankments.

Secondary Feeder Routes: Silchar–Hailakandi Road and Silchar–Kalain Road experienced multi-hour closures due to fallen utility infrastructure.

5) Compound Vulnerability Creation (Pre-Flood Context)

Utility Network Collapse: Structural damage to transformers and the high-voltage grid delayed maintenance operations right before the May monsoon onset.

Geotechnical Pre-Conditioning: Heavy pre-monsoon precipitation saturated topsoil layers across Cachar's embankments, reducing soil shear strength ahead of the June Barak River breaches.

* landslides in 2022-

A. May 2022 – Dima Hasao Cluster Landslides (Affecting Silchar Connectivity)
Dates: 11–18 May 2022.

Area: Dima Hasao district (Haflong town and surrounding hills), on the main hill route to Silchar.

Trigger: Heavy rainfall:

Up to 156 mm in 24 hours at Haflong on 11 May 2022.

About 200 mm of antecedent rainfall in 4 days, saturating slopes and triggering failures.

Scale: 5,178 landslides mapped via satellite imagery across Dima Hasao during this event.

Casualties: 3 landslide deaths reported in Dima Hasao.

Roads affected:

Lumding–Silchar highway damaged at multiple places by landslides and debris flows.

Lumding–Badarpur rail section blocked by landslides and waterlogging; 2 trains stranded, around 2,800 passengers evacuated.

New Haflong railway station – platforms and tracks buried by multiple debris flows (simulations suggest around 4 m of debris accumulation), disrupting rail services to Silchar.

People affected:

By 17 May 2022, 32,959 people evacuated across affected Assam districts.

Haflong / Dima Hasao had access problems due to landslides.

2,887 houses damaged statewide (many in hill areas) by 17 May 2022.

Districts affected (flood + landslide): 20 districts, including Cachar and Dima Hasao, under flood/landslide stress in this phase.

B. April–May 2022 – Barak Valley Landslides (Cachar, Karimganj, Hailakandi)
Period: Pre‑monsoon 2022 (heavy showers).

Districts: Cachar (Silchar district), Karimganj, Hailakandi (Barak Valley).

Casualties:

At least 21 landslide deaths in Barak Valley in one major spell.

Cachar (Lakhipur): 7 deaths – e.g. Tolapur village (debris fell on houses).

Hailakandi: 8 deaths – e.g. Mohonpur reserved area, Bowarthal.

Karimganj: 6 deaths – e.g. Barthal area.

Affected areas (villages / localities):

Cachar: Tolapur village and surrounding areas in Lakhipur revenue circle.

Hailakandi: Mohonpur reserved area (Hailakandi circle) and Bowarthal (Algapur circle).

Karimganj: Barthal and surrounding hill villages.

Roads affected:

Rural and inter‑district roads connecting:

Silchar – Lakhipur – hill areas

Silchar – Hailakandi

Silchar – Karimganj (via hilly stretches)
faced landslide debris, fallen trees, and temporary blockages.

Lumding–Silchar highway repeatedly damaged and blocked by landslides, affecting supply routes into Silchar.

C. June–July 2022 – Flood‑Related Landslides (Assam & Barak Valley)
Period: June–July 2022 (second flood wave).

Statewide landslide deaths: 18 of 179 total flood/landslide deaths in Assam by early July 2022 were due to landslides.

District‑wise landslide deaths (2022 cumulative, official):

Cachar: 3 landslide deaths (plus 45 flood deaths).

Dima Hasao: 4 landslide deaths.

Hailakandi: 2 landslide deaths.

Karimganj: 2 landslide deaths.

Infrastructure impact:

5,370 roads affected statewide (PWD + rural roads) during the 2022 flood–landslide season.

543 road breaches and numerous culvert/bridge damages reported, many in hilly and semi‑hilly stretches that feed into Silchar.

Connectivity to Silchar:

Repeated landslide‑related disruptions on hill highways (especially Lumding–Silchar and other Barak Valley hill roads) caused supply shortages and price rises for vegetables and grains in Cachar, Karimganj, and Hailakandi
